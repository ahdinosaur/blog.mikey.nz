Before I became a parent, I felt I had plateued with personal development. I wanted a new challenge. Parenting delivered.

<!-- more -->

I went all-in on being a parent, I gave my 100%. I'm very proud of who I've become and what I've learned.

I'm only 3 years into this journey, with a nearly 3-year-old and another soon to be born.

Over this journey, I've developed some opinions. Make of these opinions as you will. I'm not saying they are the correct opinions, but they are mine, and I stand by them.

## My Parenting School of Thought

As I became a parent, I realized there are so many schools of thought when it comes to parenting styles.

They all have something to offer, they each come from a good place, but still I'm not sure I'm on board with how I see them used.

I often joke that I'm not a gentle parent, because I see those who I belive subscribe to the school of "gentle parenting", doing things I wouldn't do, and not doing things I do. But I might be incorrect in my understanding, and it's all just labels anyway.

At a high-level, we could think in terms of boundaries and empathy:

- Authoritarian parenting: high boundaries, low empathy
- Permissive parenting: low boundaries, high empathy
- Authoritative parenting: high boundaries, high empathy

The best, in my opinion but also backed by study, is authoritative parenting.

I learned this term only recently, by reading [a comment on Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/AITAH/s/iIXkpzIsbn) which nearly described a situation exactly like I'd parent:

> "Before we put on Bluey we need to remember something honey. We can only watch 1, 2 or 3 episodes. How many are we going to watch?"
>
> "3!"
>
> "Okay honey, but once the third episode is over we have to turn it of. Deal?"
>
> "Yes."
>
> "Shake on it?"

> _as episode 3 is starting, I make sure to physically touch the child to get their attention_
>
> "Remember kiddo, this is the last episode. When this is over we turn of the telly. Right?"
>
> "Right"
>
> "Okay, episode is over. Now we turn of the telly, do you want to do it or shall I?"
>
> "NOOOOOOOOO!!!"
>
> "Oh honey, was it difficult to turn of Bluey? Yeah I know, it is really fun. I'm also sometimes sad to turn off the tv."
>
> " loud shrieking "
>
> "I see this is hard for you. You are angry. Do you want a cuddle?"
>
> " more shrieking "
>
> "Want to shake it out? Shake your arms and legs, shake it out shake it out. Shake that anger out."

What I've learned is about
So many terms: gentle parenting, attachment parenting

- authoritarian vs permissive, empathetic vs non-empathetic.
- best is authoritarian and empathetic
- https://www.reddit.com/r/AITAH/s/iIXkpzIsbn
- but when in a loop, skip to authority, avoid looping in empathy.

## Baby-led vs Parent-led

- baby-led weening is great, but requires patience.
- but at the same time, not every tamtrum needs a conversation before proceeding, sometimes it's best to force a blocked situation to get to the next stage.
- similarly, i believe in exposure therapy. regularly pushing a kid beyond their comfort limits, with love and care, without guilt.
- if they have a negative association, i expose them to the negative a little bit every day, calmly and consistently. e.g. washing hair in the bath

## Conclusion

Every kid is different, every parent is different. I believe if you truly are doing your best, there are no wrong answers. Don't aim for perfection, just good enough. Be yourself, be confident in yourself.
